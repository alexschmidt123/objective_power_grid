\begin{abstract}
This paper studies sequential Bayesian experimental design for uncertain
power-grid dynamics. At each stage, a policy selects a probing bus and duration,
observes a noisy frequency response, and updates uncertainty over
machine-specific parameters. We distinguish parameter-oriented expected
information gain (EIG) from Yoon's mean objective cost of uncertainty (MOCU).
We specialize the MOCU framework to a finite operational loss combining
injection cost and unmet control requirement. Its Bayesian terminal decision
is the posterior $(1-\alpha)$ quantile, where $\alpha=1/\lambda$, and MOCU is expected regret relative to the
model-specific optimum under that same loss.
Random, fixed, myopic,
deep adaptive design (DAD), and reinforcement-learning sequential Bayesian
optimal experimental design (RL-sBOED), and Step-DAD form the comparison under common
simulation banks and held-out protocols. A susceptible--infected--recovered (SIR) model
provides an implementation benchmark; IEEE 9-bus, IEEE 14-bus, and IEEE 30-bus
systems define the planned application sequence. IEEE30 dynamic implementation
and the final multi-seed comparisons remain to be completed. \todo{Add final
quantitative findings.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{U}{ncertainty} in power-system inertia and fast frequency response
complicates frequency-security assessment and control selection. Controlled
probing can reduce uncertainty, but an experiment must determine where to probe,
how long to probe, and whether later probes should depend on earlier
observations. \todo{Add motivation and citations.}

EIG values parameter learning, but parameter information does not necessarily
improve a final control decision. We therefore also study MOCU, which values an
experiment by its reduction of expected decision penalty.

The study is organized around the following intended contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item sequential bus--duration BOED for uncertain power-grid dynamics;
  \item a power-grid specialization of Yoon MOCU linking probing to the excess
  operational loss of a posterior-quantile control;
  \item a matched comparison of random, fixed, myopic, DAD, RL-sBOED, and
  Step-DAD; and
  \item SIR, IEEE9, IEEE14, and IEEE30 studies of objective choice, adaptation,
  horizon, and scalability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

The formulation follows the physical and decision-making workflow: (i) define
the grid dynamics and latent parameter space; (ii) define the reset probing
experiment, design space, observation model, and posterior; (iii) define EIG
for parameter learning; (iv) define the practical terminal control and safety
standards; and (v) construct MOCU from the same control cost and safety
quantities. Policy optimization is introduced only after these objects are
fixed.

\subsection{Power-Grid Dynamics and Latent Parameter Space}
Let $\mathcal N=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote all physical buses, and let
$\mathcal M\subset\mathcal N$ denote the buses containing dynamic machines.
For the reduced IEEE9 and IEEE14 models, the frequency response is modeled by
\begin{equation}
\mathbf M\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(s)
=-\Delta\mathbf P_e(s)
-\left(\frac{\mathbf K}{2\pi}+\mathbf D\right)
\boldsymbol{\omega}(s)+\mathbf u(s),
\label{eq:swing-frequency}
\end{equation}
following the inertia--frequency-response model in~\cite{peng2024probing}.
The vectors $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and $\Delta\mathbf P_e$ contain angular-
frequency deviations and network electrical-power changes at the dynamic
machines. The diagonal matrices $\mathbf M$, $\mathbf K$, and $\mathbf D$
contain inertia, fast frequency-response, and damping coefficients,
respectively, and $\mathbf u$ is an external active-power injection.

In these reduced models, only the machine-specific coefficients are uncertain:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\theta}
=\bigl((M_i,K_i):i\in\mathcal M\bigr)\in\Theta,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\theta}\sim p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta}).
\label{eq:theta-prior}
\end{equation}
Thus, every physical bus is an admissible probe location, whereas $M_i$ and
$K_i$ are defined only for $i\in\mathcal M$. The IEEE9 and IEEE14 models have,
respectively, $(N,|\mathcal M|)=(9,3)$ and $(14,5)$; their static network data
follow the standard IEEE test cases distributed with
MATPOWER~\cite{zimmerman2011matpower}.

Let $\mathbf a(b)$ map a scalar injection at physical bus $b\in\mathcal N$ to
the dynamic-machine equations. At a machine bus, the injection enters that
machine directly. At a non-machine bus, Kron reduction distributes the
injection among the dynamic machines. A non-machine bus therefore remains a
valid probe location without being assigned artificial dynamic states,
$M_i$, or $K_i$.

\subsection{Reset Probe Experiment and Design Space}
At stage $t$, the design is a bus--duration pair
\begin{equation}
\xi_t=(b_t,\tau_t)\in\Xi=\mathcal N\times\mathcal T,
\label{eq:probe-action}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ is the six-duration catalog and the amplitude $A$ is fixed.
The probing signal is the Hann pulse used for controlled frequency excitation
in~\cite{peng2024probing}:
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{A}{2}
\left[1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi s}{\tau_t}\right)\right],
&0\le s\le\tau_t,\\
0,&s>\tau_t.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hann-probe}
\end{equation}
During a probe simulation,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf u(s)=\mathbf a(b_t)u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t),
\qquad u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=0,
\label{eq:probe-test-input}
\end{equation}
and the system is reset to its configured equilibrium before the next probe.
IEEE9 and IEEE14 contain $9\times6=54$ and $14\times6=84$ actions,
respectively. Exact repetition of a bus--duration pair is disallowed within a
rollout.

After $t$ probes, the realized history is
\begin{equation}
h_t=\bigl((\xi_1,\mathbf y_1),\ldots,(\xi_t,\mathbf y_t)\bigr).
\label{eq:history}
\end{equation}
An adaptive policy selects
\begin{equation}
\xi_t\sim\pi_t(\,\cdot\mid h_{t-1}),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T,
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
subject to the no-repetition constraint. This is the standard adaptive
formulation of sequential BOED~\cite{foster2021dad}. The horizon $T$ is the
probe budget. A fixed design selects its entire sequence before any data are
observed; an adaptive design may change later actions in response to the
realized observations.

\subsubsection{Observation model and Bayesian update}
Let $o\in\mathcal M$ be the monitored PMU bus and
$\Delta f_o(s)=\omega_o(s)/(2\pi)$ its frequency deviation. At prescribed
sampling times $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}$, define the noise-free response
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y(\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)
=\bigl[\Delta f_o(s_j;\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)\bigr]_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}.
\label{eq:forward-observation}
\end{equation}
The measured response is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_t=\mathbf y(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\xi_t)
+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t,\qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\sigma_y^2\mathbf I).
\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is a configurable observation dimension rather than a
fixed part of the problem definition. Resetting and independent measurement
noise give $p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol\theta,h_{t-1},\xi_t)
=p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol\theta,\xi_t)$. The adaptive selection
probabilities depend on observed history, not additionally on the unknown
parameter, so they cancel from the posterior:
\begin{equation}
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid h_t)\propto p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})
\prod_{k=1}^{t}p(\mathbf y_k\mid\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_k).
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The implementation represents this posterior with $N_{\mathrm p}$ particles
$\{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},w_n^{(t)}\}_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}$. Their weights
are updated by
\begin{equation}
w_n^{(t)}
=\frac{w_n^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},\xi_t)}
{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}w_j^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(j)},\xi_t)}.
\label{eq:particle-update}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Expected Information Gain}
Let $H_T$ denote the random terminal history induced by policy $\pi$. For the
parameter-learning experiments, the policy maximizes
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{EIG}(\pi)
=\E_{H_T\mid\pi}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid H_T)
\Vert p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\right].
\label{eq:policy-eig}
\end{equation}
This is the expected information gain used in sequential BOED
\cite{foster2021dad,blau2022rlsboed}. It rewards posterior learning about
$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ but does not directly encode the quality of the
downstream control decision.

\subsection{Practical Control and Safety Conditions}
Diagnostic probing and terminal control are evaluated in separate simulations.
After the final probe, the operator selects a supplementary-injection magnitude
$u\in\mathcal U$. Its location, activation time, and temporal profile are fixed.
For model $\boldsymbol\theta$, contingency $c\in\mathcal C$, and magnitude $u$,
the terminal simulator returns frequency nadir
$f_{\mathrm{nad}}(\boldsymbol\theta,c,u)$, maximum absolute ROCOF
$r(\boldsymbol\theta,c,u)$. The intervention is safe for all
credible contingencies when
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Safe}(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)
\iff
\begin{cases}
f_{\mathrm{nad}}(\boldsymbol\theta,c,u)\ge f_{\mathrm{lim}},\\
r(\boldsymbol\theta,c,u)\le r_{\mathrm{lim}},
\end{cases}
\quad \text{for every }c\in\mathcal C.
\label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
The next section expresses these requirements as a feasible control set and
defines the minimum safe action. The retained parameter support must contain a
safe candidate for every particle.

\subsection{Finite-Loss Control-Oriented MOCU}

\subsubsection{Relation to Yoon's MOCU}
For a specified operational loss $L(\theta,u)$, the model-specific and
intrinsically Bayesian robust (IBR) actions are, respectively,
\begin{align}
u_\theta^*&\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}L(\theta,u),\\
u_b^*&\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u)].
\label{eq:bayesian-control}
\end{align}
Yoon et al.'s construction compares these actions using the same loss
\cite{yoon2013mocu}; the generalized formulation also treats noisy
experiments~\cite{boluki2018generalized}:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MOCU}(b)=\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u_b^*)-L(\theta,u_\theta^*)].
\label{eq:general_mocu}
\end{equation}
Bayesian robustness means minimizing expected cost, not necessarily taking a
worst-case maximum. The loss below is our power-grid specialization; neither
its penalty parameter nor the resulting quantile level is prescribed by Yoon et al.

\subsubsection{Physical control requirement and finite loss}
Let $\mathcal U=[0,u_{\max}]$ be the continuous terminal-control range. Define
$U(\theta)$ as the minimum magnitude satisfying the prescribed nadir and
ROCOF limits for every $c\in\mathcal C$. The scalar requirement model assumes
that safety is monotone over this range and that a safe magnitude exists.
These assumptions must be checked for each simulator and contingency set;
otherwise a scalar threshold is insufficient and the full simulated
action-dependent loss must be used. Equivalently, under these assumptions,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{safe}}(\theta)=[U(\theta),u_{\max}].
\label{eq:safe_control_set}
\end{equation}
The current IEEE9 scenario is the configured single contingency; a general
set $\mathcal C$ does not establish N--1 security without evaluating that set.

For $\lambda>1$ and $(z)_+=\max(z,0)$, choose
\begin{equation}
L(\theta,u)=u+\lambda[U(\theta)-u]_+.
\label{eq:operational_cost}
\end{equation}
This finite loss penalizes supplementary injection and unmet required
injection. It is a power-equivalent operational surrogate, not an empirically
calibrated monetary damage model or a direct measure of violation severity.
The dimensionless parameter $\lambda$ is the shortfall penalty relative to
the control-cost coefficient, which is normalized to one. It is prespecified
to encode an asymmetric preference against
under-control. A sensitivity study should vary this preference while changing
the decision quantile consistently. No additional binary violation penalty
or control margin is included in the primary formulation.

Since the loss decreases with slope $1-\lambda$ below $U$ and increases with
slope $1$ above $U$, its model-specific minimizer is
\begin{equation}
u_\theta^*=u_{\mathrm{optimal}}(\theta)=U(\theta),
\quad L(\theta,u_\theta^*)=U(\theta).
\label{eq:model_specific_control}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Derivation of the parameterized quantile decision}
Write $F_b(v)=\Pr_b(U\le v)$ and $\alpha=1/\lambda\in(0,1)$.
The left and right derivatives of posterior expected loss at $u$ are
$1-\lambda+\lambda F_b(u^-)$ and
$1-\lambda+\lambda F_b(u)$, respectively. Convexity therefore gives the
optimality condition
\begin{equation}
F_b(u^- )\le 1-\alpha\le F_b(u).
\label{eq:quantile_optimality}
\end{equation}
Choosing the smallest minimizer yields
\begin{equation}
u_b^*=Q_{1-\alpha}(U\mid b)
=\inf\{v:F_b(v)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\label{eq:u_ctrl_support}
\end{equation}
Thus the quantile is derived from the finite loss, rather than appended to
an unrelated MOCU definition. Equivalently, realized regret is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{OCU}(\theta,b)
=(u_b^*-U)_++(\lambda-1)(U-u_b^*)_+\ge0.
\label{eq:ocu}
\end{equation}
Over-control has unit marginal regret and under-control has marginal regret
$\lambda-1$; the coefficient $\lambda$ in the total loss includes the avoided injection cost.
The corresponding belief MOCU is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MOCU}(b)=\mathbb E_b\!\left[
u_b^*+\lambda(U-u_b^*)_+-U\right].
\label{eq:belief_mocu}
\end{equation}
It vanishes when the required control is known exactly, even if other model
parameters remain uncertain. Subtracting $\mathbb E_b[U]$ without including
the shortfall term is not this finite-loss MOCU.

\subsubsection{Particle bank and implemented terminal action}
Let $U_n$ denote the stored control requirement for particle $\theta^{(n)}$,
with normalized posterior weights $w_n$. The empirical CDF and decision are
\begin{align}
F_N(v)&=\sum_n w_n\mathbf1\{U_n\le v\},\\
q_N&=\inf\{v:F_N(v)\ge1-\alpha\},\\
u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(b)&=\operatorname{snap}_{\mathcal G}^{\uparrow}(q_N).
\label{eq:u_ctrl}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal G$ is the configured control grid. The aligned implementation
requires every $U_n\in\mathcal G$ and zero margin, so the empirical quantile
already lies on the grid and snapping does not change it. It is consequently
an exact IBR action for this finite particle-bank loss. If requirements lie
off-grid, rounding a continuous quantile upward is not generally the
unconstrained grid Bayes optimum; one must minimize expected loss over the
grid explicitly or retain continuous controls. The implementation checks
this alignment before running the primary experiment.

The bank-level objective used for training and posterior evaluation is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{MOCU}}_N(b)
=\sum_n w_n\{u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}+\lambda(U_n-u_{\mathrm{ctrl}})_+-U_n\}.
\label{eq:particle_mocu}
\end{equation}
All methods use the same bank, loss, quantile, and control range. Probe and
control bank rows must match in particle values and ordering, physics,
scenario, units, and simulator provenance. A dense control-grid bank is a
numerical approximation to the continuous physical requirements; its error
must be distinguished from posterior uncertainty.

\subsubsection{Sequential design and held-out validation}
The ideal design objective and its implemented approximation are
\begin{align}
\pi^*&\in\arg\min_\pi\mathbb E_{H_T\mid\pi}
[\operatorname{MOCU}(b_T)],\\
\widehat\pi&\approx\arg\min_\pi\mathbb E_{H_T\mid\pi}
[\widehat{\operatorname{MOCU}}_N(b_T)].
\label{eq:mocu_policy}
\end{align}
Under an exact posterior and the same generative model, the tower property
equates expected posterior MOCU to expected realized regret. Also,
$\mathbb E_{H_T}\mathbb E_{b_T}[U]=\mathbb E_{p_0}[U]$ is independent of the
probe policy: minimizing expected MOCU is equivalent to minimizing expected
total operational loss, not merely the control magnitude. Optimal expected
MOCU cannot increase when additional observations may be ignored; this does
not imply monotonic improvement for every realized history or trained policy.

For an independent test system $\theta^\star$, evaluate the applied action
against a separately computed continuous physical oracle $U^*(\theta^\star)$:
\begin{equation}
R^*=u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}+\lambda[U^*(\theta^\star)-u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}]_+
-U^*(\theta^\star).
\label{eq:realized_regret}
\end{equation}
Mean held-out $R^*$ is the primary empirical endpoint; posterior bank MOCU
is reported separately. Their difference can reflect finite support,
control-grid approximation, and model or posterior miscalibration. Regret
against a continuous oracle includes control discretization error and should
not be described as purely uncertainty cost for a restricted grid action
class. A strictly grid-relative MOCU instead subtracts
$\min_{v\in\mathcal G}L(\theta,v)$ for each model.

Under the threshold model and exact posterior, the quantile ensures posterior
coverage $\Pr_b(U\le u_b^*)\ge1-\alpha$. It does not protect every posterior
model and is not a guaranteed empirical safety rate. Report true-system
nadir and ROCOF violations, safety counts and confidence intervals, control
magnitude, and regret separately. Any 95\% safety eligibility rule must be
prespecified; retain results for ineligible methods and distinguish a point
estimate passing 95\% from statistical evidence for that claim. The finite
loss is scientifically a MOCU specialization, while its engineering adequacy
depends on the stated loss preference, physical validation, and calibration.

\section{Discussion}
Discuss when adaptation and non-myopic planning improve the downstream
objective, why Fixed can be competitive, why EIG and MOCU can disagree, and how
horizon and system size affect performance. Limitations include simulated
Kron-reduced networks, prescribed priors, fixed probe amplitude, discrete
actions, simplified terminal control, and three training seeds. Reduced-model $K_i$ allocations and parameter priors are study assumptions.
The paper-based IEEE30 model requires a different dynamic representation.
The finite-loss coefficient needs sensitivity analysis and does not quantify
actual outage damage; empirical calibration and physical safety must be
validated separately. Adaptive branching and improvement over Myopic and
Fixed are empirical hypotheses, not prerequisites imposed on the results.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper formulates power-grid probing as sequential BOED and connects
learning to a finite-loss Bayesian terminal decision using Yoon's MOCU
construction. The quantile level $1-1/\lambda$ follows from the chosen loss, while
