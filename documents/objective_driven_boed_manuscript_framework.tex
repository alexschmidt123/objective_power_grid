\begin{abstract}
This paper studies sequential Bayesian experimental design for uncertain
power-grid dynamics. At each stage, a policy selects a probing bus and duration,
observes a noisy frequency response, and updates uncertainty over
machine-specific parameters. We distinguish parameter-oriented expected
information gain (EIG) from a goal-oriented specialization of Yoon's
multi-objective mean objective cost of uncertainty (MOCU). The operational
cost combines supplementary injection with the probability and severity of
violating frequency-nadir, ROCOF, and settling requirements. The familiar
hard-safety IBR expression is retained as a reference special case of the same
MOCU theory.
Random, fixed, myopic,
deep adaptive design (DAD), and reinforcement-learning sequential Bayesian
optimal experimental design (RL-sBOED), and Step-DAD are evaluated under common
simulation banks and held-out protocols. A susceptible--infected--recovered (SIR) model
provides an implementation benchmark; IEEE 9-bus, IEEE 14-bus, and IEEE 30-bus
systems assess the power-grid application and scalability. \todo{Add final
quantitative findings.}

EIG values parameter learning, but parameter information does not necessarily

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Power-Grid Model}
Let $\mathcal N=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote all physical buses, and let
$\mathcal M\subset\mathcal N$ denote the buses containing dynamic machines.
The frequency response of those machines is modeled by
\begin{equation}
\mathbf M\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(s)
=-\Delta\mathbf P_e(s)
-\left(\frac{\mathbf K}{2\pi}+\mathbf D\right)
\boldsymbol{\omega}(s)+\mathbf u(s),
\label{eq:swing-frequency}
\end{equation}
following the inertia--frequency-response model in~\cite{peng2024probing}.
The vectors $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and $\Delta\mathbf P_e$ contain angular-
frequency deviations and network electrical-power changes at the dynamic
machines. The diagonal matrices $\mathbf M$, $\mathbf K$, and $\mathbf D$
contain inertia, fast frequency-response, and damping coefficients,
respectively, and $\mathbf u$ is an external active-power injection.

Only the machine-specific coefficients are uncertain:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\theta}
=\bigl((M_i,K_i):i\in\mathcal M\bigr)\in\Theta,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\theta}\sim p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta}).
\label{eq:theta-prior}
\end{equation}
Thus, every physical bus is an admissible probe location, whereas $M_i$ and
$K_i$ are defined only for $i\in\mathcal M$. The IEEE9 and IEEE14 models have,
respectively, $(N,|\mathcal M|)=(9,3)$ and $(14,5)$; their static network data
follow the standard IEEE test cases distributed with
MATPOWER~\cite{zimmerman2011matpower}.

Let $\mathbf a(b)$ map a scalar injection at physical bus $b\in\mathcal N$ to
the dynamic-machine equations. At a machine bus, the injection enters that
machine directly. At a non-machine bus, Kron reduction distributes the
injection among the dynamic machines. A non-machine bus therefore remains a
valid probe location without being assigned artificial dynamic states,
$M_i$, or $K_i$.

\subsection{Sequential Probe Design}
At stage $t$, the design is a bus--duration pair
\begin{equation}
\xi_t=(b_t,\tau_t)\in\Xi=\mathcal N\times\mathcal T,
\label{eq:probe-action}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ is the six-duration catalog and the amplitude $A$ is fixed.
The probing signal is the Hann pulse used for controlled frequency excitation
in~\cite{peng2024probing}:
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{A}{2}
\left[1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi s}{\tau_t}\right)\right],
&0\le s\le\tau_t,\\
0,&s>\tau_t.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hann-probe}
\end{equation}
During a probe simulation,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf u(s)=\mathbf a(b_t)u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t),
\qquad u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=0,
\label{eq:probe-test-input}
\end{equation}
and the system is reset to its configured equilibrium before the next probe.
IEEE9 and IEEE14 contain $9\times6=54$ and $14\times6=84$ actions,
respectively. Exact repetition of a bus--duration pair is disallowed within a
rollout.

After $t$ probes, the realized history is
\begin{equation}
h_t=\bigl((\xi_1,\mathbf y_1),\ldots,(\xi_t,\mathbf y_t)\bigr).
\label{eq:history}
\end{equation}
An adaptive policy selects
\begin{equation}
\xi_t\sim\pi_t(\,\cdot\mid h_{t-1}),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T,
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
subject to the no-repetition constraint. This is the standard adaptive
formulation of sequential BOED~\cite{foster2021dad}. The horizon $T$ is the
probe budget. A fixed design selects its entire sequence before any data are
observed; an adaptive design may change later actions in response to the
realized observations.

\subsection{Observation Model and Bayesian Update}
Let $o\in\mathcal M$ be the fixed PMU bus and
$\Delta f_o(s)=\omega_o(s)/(2\pi)$ its frequency deviation. For sampling set
$\mathcal S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}\}$, the simulator output is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf g(\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)
=\bigl[\Delta f_o(s;\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)\bigr]_{s\in\mathcal S}.
\label{eq:forward-observation}
\end{equation}
The measured response is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_t=\mathbf g(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\xi_t)
+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t,\qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\sigma_y^2\mathbf I).
\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
The main power-grid experiments use $N_{\mathrm{obs}}=5$. Because every probe
starts from equilibrium, the responses are conditionally independent given
$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and the selected designs:
\begin{equation}
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid h_t)\propto p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})
\prod_{k=1}^{t}p(\mathbf y_k\mid\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_k).
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The implementation represents this posterior with $N_{\mathrm p}$ particles
$\{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},w_n^{(t)}\}_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}$. Their weights
are updated by
\begin{equation}
w_n^{(t)}
=\frac{w_n^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},\xi_t)}
{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}w_j^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(j)},\xi_t)}.
\label{eq:particle-update}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Parameter-Oriented Design Objective}
Let $H_T$ denote the random terminal history induced by policy $\pi$. For the
parameter-learning experiments, the policy maximizes
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{EIG}}(\pi)
=\E_{H_T\mid\pi}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid H_T)
\Vert p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\right].
\label{eq:policy-eig}
\end{equation}
This is the expected information gain used in sequential BOED
\cite{foster2021dad,blau2022rlsboed}. It rewards posterior learning about
$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ but does not directly encode the quality of the
downstream control decision.

\subsection{Terminal Control and Safety}
Diagnostic probing and terminal-control evaluation are separate simulations.
In the latter, the probe is zero and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf u(s)=\mathbf a(b_c)u_{\mathrm c}(s)
+\mathbf a(b_u)u\,h_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(s),
\qquad u_{\mathrm p}=0,
\label{eq:terminal-control-input}
\end{equation}
where $u_{\mathrm c}$ is the fixed contingency, $u\in\mathcal U$ is a candidate
supplementary control magnitude, and $b_c$ and $b_u$ are their configured
locations.

For parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and control $u$, let
$r(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)$ be the maximum absolute rate of change of frequency
(ROCOF), and let $f_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)$ be the minimum frequency
deviation. A control is safe when
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Safe}(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)
\iff
r(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)\le r_{\max}
\ \text{and}\
f_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)\ge\underline f.
\label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
For the ordered admissible grid $\mathcal U$, define the required control
\begin{equation}
U(\boldsymbol{\theta})=
\min\{u\in\mathcal U:\mathsf{Safe}(\boldsymbol{\theta},u)\}.
\label{eq:required-control}
\end{equation}
The retained parameter support is required to contain a safe candidate. The
offline bank stores $U_n=U(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)})$ for each particle.

Given terminal weights $\mathbf w^{(T)}$, define the numerically active
posterior support
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_T=\{n:w_n^{(T)}>\varepsilon_w\},
\qquad \varepsilon_w=10^{-12}.
\label{eq:active-support}
\end{equation}
If this set is empty because of numerical underflow, all particles with
strictly positive normalized weight are retained. Yoon's IBR operator and the
deployed practical control action are
\begin{equation}
u_T=u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(h_T)
=\psi^{\mathrm{IBR}}(h_T)
=\max_{n\in\mathcal I_T}U_n.
\label{eq:posterior-control}
\end{equation}
This is the hardest model-specific optimum remaining on the numerical
posterior support. A posterior-quantile controller is distinct from this Yoon
IBR baseline. A chance constraint is, however, part of the proposed risk-aware
decision in Eq.~\eqref{eq:risk-aware-control}.

\subsection{Yoon IBR--MOCU Baseline}
MOCU measures the expected performance loss caused by uncertainty relative to
model-specific operation~\cite{yoon2013mocu}. For particle $n$, Yoon's
objective cost of uncertainty under the hard-safety IBR operator is
\begin{equation}
\ocu_n(h_T)
=C_{\boldsymbol\theta^{(n)}}(u_T)
-C_{\boldsymbol\theta^{(n)}}(U_n)
=u_T-U_n\ge 0,
\label{eq:ocu}
\end{equation}
for every particle in the active posterior support. The posterior belief MOCU
is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mocu(h_T)=
\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}w_n^{(T)}
\bigl(u_T-U_n\bigr)
=u_T-\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}w_n^{(T)}U_n.
\label{eq:belief-mocu}
\end{equation}
The same active-support rule and threshold are used by the CPU evaluator and
GPU trainer. The action $u_T$ is the physical command applied in the terminal
contingency simulation; MOCU is its posterior expected excess cost relative to
model-specific operation. Physical safety on held-out systems is reported
separately because a finite particle approximation cannot guarantee coverage
of an off-support true system. This baseline measures excess injection after
imposing hard safety; it cannot grade the posterior probability or severity of
unsafe off-support outcomes and is not the proposed primary objective.

\subsection{Risk-Aware Multi-Objective MOCU Specialization}
For a model $\boldsymbol\theta$ and terminal injection $u$, define normalized
stability loss
\begin{align}
S(\boldsymbol\theta,u)
={}&w_f\left[\frac{\underline f-f_{\min}(\boldsymbol\theta,u)}{s_f}\right]_+
+w_r\left[\frac{r(\boldsymbol\theta,u)-r_{\max}}{s_r}\right]_+ \notag\\
&+w_t\left[\frac{t_{\mathrm{set}}(\boldsymbol\theta,u)-\bar t_{\mathrm{set}}}{s_t}\right]_+,
\label{eq:stability-loss}
\end{align}
where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and the scales make the terms dimensionless. The
Following Yoon's general and multi-objective MOCU
formulations~\cite{yoon2013mocu,yoon2021multimocu}, define the model-dependent
operational cost
\begin{equation}
\xi_{\boldsymbol\theta}(u;\boldsymbol\lambda)=
\lambda_c\widetilde C_u(u)
+\lambda_0\mathbf 1\{S(\boldsymbol\theta,u)>0\}
+\lambda_s S(\boldsymbol\theta,u).
\label{eq:risk-loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\widetilde C_u$ is a normalized monetary or resource cost, potentially
nonlinear. The nonnegative weight vector $\boldsymbol\lambda=(\lambda_c,
\lambda_0,\lambda_s)$ is specified before method comparison. This is exactly
the combined operational-cost construction of multi-objective MOCU, not a new
definition of MOCU. The IBR practical command and model-specific optimum are
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(b;\boldsymbol\lambda)
=\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}
\E_{\boldsymbol\theta\sim b}
[\xi_{\boldsymbol\theta}(u;\boldsymbol\lambda)],
\qquad
u_{\boldsymbol\theta}^*(\boldsymbol\lambda)
=\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}
\xi_{\boldsymbol\theta}(u;\boldsymbol\lambda).
\label{eq:risk-aware-control}
\end{equation}
Substitution into Yoon's definition gives the risk-aware power-grid MOCU
\begin{equation}
\mocu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}(b)=
\E_{\boldsymbol\theta\sim b}\!\left[
\xi_{\boldsymbol\theta}(u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(b;\boldsymbol\lambda);\boldsymbol\lambda)
-\xi_{\boldsymbol\theta}(u_{\boldsymbol\theta}^*(\boldsymbol\lambda);\boldsymbol\lambda)
\right].
\label{eq:risk-aware-mocu}
\end{equation}
It is the additional economic and stability-risk cost caused by uncertainty;
raw $u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ alone is not a cost-of-uncertainty measure. The primary
$\boldsymbol\lambda$ is fixed from operational requirements, and a declared
weight sensitivity analysis reports the cost--risk frontier. CVaR is reported
only as a secondary tail-risk diagnostic and is not inserted into the primary
MOCU definition. Safety probability, violation severity, injection cost, and
$u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ are reported separately so a scalar score cannot hide a
safety--cost tradeoff.

The objective-driven policy is
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\mathrm{MOCU}}^\star
=\arg\min_{\pi}\;
\E_{H_T\mid\pi}\!\left[
\mocu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\bigl(p(\boldsymbol\theta\mid H_T)\bigr)\right],
\qquad |H_T|=T.
\label{eq:policy-mocu}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, probing maximizes expected prior-to-terminal MOCU reduction.
Lower terminal MOCU is better. Unlike EIG, this goal-oriented BOED objective
values parameter learning only when it improves the downstream cost--risk
decision.
