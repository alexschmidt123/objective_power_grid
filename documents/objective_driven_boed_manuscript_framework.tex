\begin{abstract}
This paper studies sequential Bayesian experimental design for uncertain
power-grid dynamics. At each stage, a policy selects a probing bus and duration,
observes a noisy frequency response, and updates uncertainty over
machine-specific parameters. We distinguish parameter-oriented expected
information gain (EIG) from Yoon's mean objective cost of uncertainty (MOCU).
We specialize the MOCU framework to a finite operational loss combining
injection cost and unmet control requirement. Its Bayesian terminal decision
is the posterior $(1-\alpha)$ quantile, where $\alpha=1/\lambda$, and MOCU is expected regret relative to the
model-specific optimum under that same loss.
Random, fixed, myopic,
deep adaptive design (DAD), and reinforcement-learning sequential Bayesian
optimal experimental design (RL-sBOED), and Step-DAD form the comparison under common
simulation banks and held-out protocols. A susceptible--infected--recovered (SIR) model
provides an implementation benchmark; IEEE 9-bus, IEEE 14-bus, and IEEE 30-bus
systems define the planned application sequence. IEEE30 dynamic implementation
and the final multi-seed comparisons remain to be completed. \todo{Add final
quantitative findings.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{U}{ncertainty} in power-system inertia and fast frequency response
complicates frequency-security assessment and control selection. Controlled
probing can reduce uncertainty, but an experiment must determine where to probe,
how long to probe, and whether later probes should depend on earlier
observations. \todo{Add motivation and citations.}

EIG values parameter learning, but parameter information does not necessarily
improve a final control decision. We therefore also study MOCU, which values an
experiment by its reduction of expected decision penalty.

The study is organized around the following intended contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item sequential bus--duration BOED for uncertain power-grid dynamics;
  \item a power-grid specialization of Yoon MOCU linking probing to the excess
  operational loss of a posterior-quantile control;
  \item a matched comparison of random, fixed, myopic, DAD, RL-sBOED, and
  Step-DAD; and
  \item SIR, IEEE9, IEEE14, and IEEE30 studies of objective choice, adaptation,
  horizon, and scalability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Power-Grid Dynamics and Latent Parameter Space}
Following Peng et al.~\cite{peng2024probing}, we model inertia and fast
frequency response jointly. Let $\Delta\omega(s)$ be angular-speed deviation
from an equilibrium with synchronous speed $\omega_{\mathrm s}=2\pi f_0$.
On a common power base $S_{\mathrm{base}}$, the incremental power balance is
\begin{equation}
M\frac{d\Delta\omega}{ds}
=\Delta P_m-\Delta P_e-D\Delta\omega,
\qquad M=\frac{2HS_n}{\omega_{\mathrm s}S_{\mathrm{base}}}.
\label{eq:inertia-balance}
\end{equation}
Here $H$ is the inertia constant, $S_n$ the rating associated with $H$,
$\Delta P_m$ and $\Delta P_e$ the input and electrical-output power changes,
and $D$ the damping coefficient. Power changes are in per unit.
The paper represents fast frequency support by a droop relation,
\begin{equation}
\Delta P_m=-K\Delta f=-\frac{K}{2\pi}\Delta\omega,
\qquad \Delta f=\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi},
\label{eq:frequency-support}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is expressed here in per-unit power per Hz. Substitution gives
\begin{equation}
M\frac{d\Delta\omega}{ds}
=-\Delta P_e-\left(\frac{K}{2\pi}+D\right)\Delta\omega.
\label{eq:aggregate_model}
\end{equation}
Thus $M$ governs the rate of frequency change, whereas $K$ governs the
restoring power supplied in response to a frequency deviation. We retain
known damping $D$; Peng et al. neglect it in their estimation model.

To represent probe location, we extend this aggregate relation to a reduced
network. Let $\mathcal N$ be the physical-bus set and
$\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal N$ the dynamic-machine buses, with
$m=|\mathcal M|$. For $i\in\mathcal M$, write $\omega_i$ for angular-speed
deviation and $\delta_i$ for rotor angle in the synchronous frame:
\begin{align}
\dot\delta_i(s)&=\omega_i(s),\\
M_i\dot\omega_i(s)&=-\Delta P_{e,i}(\boldsymbol\delta(s))
-\left(\frac{K_i}{2\pi}+D_i\right)\omega_i(s)+v_i(s).
\label{eq:swing-frequency}
\end{align}
The initial state is $(\boldsymbol\delta^0,\mathbf0)$, and the reduced
lossless-network power change is
\begin{equation}
\Delta P_{e,i}(\boldsymbol\delta)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}B_{ij}
\left[\sin(\delta_i-\delta_j)-\sin(\delta_i^0-\delta_j^0)\right],
\label{eq:network-power}
\end{equation}
with known coupling coefficients $B_{ij}$. A physical-bus injection is
mapped to $\mathbf v$ by the known vector $\mathbf a(b)$; the reduction is
given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kron-map}. This machine-wise model is our extension
of the paper's aggregate response, with $K_i$ representing effective fast
frequency support at each retained machine bus.

The latent vector contains the inertia and response coefficients:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\theta=(M_1,\ldots,M_m,K_1,\ldots,K_m)^\top\in\Theta,
\qquad\boldsymbol\theta\sim p_0,
\label{eq:theta-prior}
\end{equation}
where indices $1,\ldots,m$ follow the retained-machine ordering and
\begin{equation}
\Theta=\prod_{i=1}^{m}[M_i^-,M_i^+]
\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}[K_i^-,K_i^+],
\qquad M_i^->0,\quad K_i^-\ge0.
\label{eq:latent-space}
\end{equation}
The topology, coupling, damping, and equilibrium are known. Algebraic buses
remain admissible probe locations but have no independent $M_i$ or $K_i$.
With bounded piecewise-continuous inputs, these assumptions define the
frequency trajectories used by the observation and terminal-control models.

\subsection{Reset Probe Experiment and Design Space}
At stage $t$, the design is a bus--duration pair
\begin{equation}
\xi_t=(b_t,\tau_t)\in\Xi=\mathcal N\times\mathcal T,
\label{eq:probe-action}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T\subset(0,S_{\mathrm p}]$ is a finite duration catalog,
$S_{\mathrm p}>0$ is the probe observation horizon, and amplitude $A$ is fixed.
The probing signal is the Hann pulse used for controlled frequency excitation
in~\cite{peng2024probing}:
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{A}{2}
\left[1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi s}{\tau_t}\right)\right],
&0\le s\le\tau_t,\\
0,&s>\tau_t.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hann-probe}
\end{equation}
During a probe simulation,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v(s)=\mathbf a(b_t)u_{\mathrm p}(s;\tau_t),
\qquad u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=0,
\label{eq:probe-test-input}
\end{equation}
and the state is reset to $(\boldsymbol\delta^0,\mathbf0)$ before each probe.
The action catalog has $|\mathcal N||\mathcal T|$ elements; assume
$T\le|\Xi|$. Exact repetition of a bus--duration pair is disallowed within a
rollout.

After $t$ probes, the realized history is
\begin{equation}
h_t=\bigl((\xi_1,\mathbf y_1),\ldots,(\xi_t,\mathbf y_t)\bigr).
\label{eq:history}
\end{equation}
An adaptive policy selects
\begin{equation}
\xi_t\sim\pi_t(\,\cdot\mid h_{t-1}),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T,
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
subject to the no-repetition constraint. The horizon $T$ is the probe budget.

\subsubsection{Observation model and Bayesian update}
Let $o\in\mathcal M$ be the monitored PMU bus and
$\Delta f_o(s)=\omega_o(s)/(2\pi)$ its frequency deviation. At prescribed
sampling times $0\le s_1<\cdots<s_{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}\le S_{\mathrm p}$,
fixed before observing outcomes, define the noise-free response
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y(\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)
=\bigl[\Delta f_o(s_j;\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_t)\bigr]_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}.
\label{eq:forward-observation}
\end{equation}
The measured response is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf y_t=\mathbf y(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\xi_t)
+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t,\qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\sigma_y^2\mathbf I).
\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is a configurable observation dimension rather than a
fixed part of the problem definition. Assume $\sigma_y>0$ (in Hz), and
noise vectors independent across probes and independent of $\theta$ and
policy randomization. For $N_{\mathrm{obs}}=1$, the single sampling time
remains a specified part of the observation model. Resetting and independent measurement
noise give $p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol\theta,h_{t-1},\xi_t)
=p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol\theta,\xi_t)$. The adaptive selection
probabilities depend on observed history, not additionally on the unknown
parameter, so they cancel from the posterior:
\begin{equation}
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid h_t)\propto p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})
\prod_{k=1}^{t}p(\mathbf y_k\mid\boldsymbol{\theta},\xi_k).
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Expected Information Gain}
Let $H_T$ denote the random terminal history induced by policy $\pi$. For the
parameter-learning experiments, the policy maximizes
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{EIG}(\pi)
=\E_{H_T\mid\pi}\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
p(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid H_T)
\Vert p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\right].
\label{eq:policy-eig}
\end{equation}
The MOCU objective below instead incorporates the terminal control loss.

\subsection{Terminal Control and Safety Constraints}
After $T$ probes, the operator uses the learned parameter distribution to
choose a corrective power injection $u\in\mathcal U=[0,u_{\max}]$.
The injection location $b_{\mathrm c}$ and time profile $g(s)$ are fixed;
only its magnitude is chosen. For a disturbance $c\in\mathcal C$, the input is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v_c(s;u)=\mathbf d_c(s)+\mathbf a(b_{\mathrm c})u g(s),
\label{eq:terminal-input}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf d_c$ is the disturbance profile and $\mathcal C$ is the
specified contingency set. Each response starts from the stated equilibrium.

A control is safe when it limits both the frequency drop and the rate of
frequency change. Over the monitored machines $\mathcal O_{\mathrm s}$ and
time interval $[0,S_{\mathrm c}]$, define
\begin{align}
f_{\mathrm{nad}}(\theta,c,u)&=
\min_{i\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm s},\,0\le s\le S_{\mathrm c}}
\Delta f_i(s;\theta,c,u),\\
r(\theta,c,u)&=
\max_{i\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm s}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0\le s\le S_{\mathrm c}}
|\dot{\Delta f}_i(s;\theta,c,u)|.
\label{eq:safety-functionals}
\end{align}
Here $\Delta f_i=\omega_i/(2\pi)$ is frequency deviation in Hz. The prescribed
nadir and ROCOF limits are $f_{\mathrm{lim}}<0$ and $r_{\mathrm{lim}}>0$:
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{Safe}(\theta,u)\iff
\begin{cases}
f_{\mathrm{nad}}(\theta,c,u)\ge f_{\mathrm{lim}},\\
r(\theta,c,u)\le r_{\mathrm{lim}},
\end{cases}
\quad\forall c\in\mathcal C.
\label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
Assume that a safe magnitude exists for every $\theta$ and that the safe set
is closed and upward closed within $\mathcal U$. Its minimum is
\begin{equation}
U(\theta)=\min\{u\in\mathcal U:\mathsf{Safe}(\theta,u)\},
\quad\mathcal U_{\mathrm{safe}}(\theta)=[U(\theta),u_{\max}].
\label{eq:safe_control_set}
\end{equation}
Assume $U$ is measurable. Feasibility and monotonicity are assumptions of
this scalar-control model. If $\theta$ were known, $U(\theta)$ would be the
smallest safe injection. Because $\theta$ is uncertain, the operator must
balance control expenditure against the possibility of choosing too little
control. The following MOCU loss specifies that tradeoff.

\subsubsection{Operational loss and objective cost of uncertainty}
Let $b$ denote a posterior probability distribution on $\Theta$.
For an operational loss $L(\theta,u)$, define the model-specific optimum
and the intrinsically Bayesian robust (IBR) action by
\begin{align}
u_\theta^*&\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}L(\theta,u),\\
u_b^*&\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U}\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u)].
\label{eq:bayesian-control}
\end{align}
For integrable costs and attained minima, MOCU is
\begin{align}
\operatorname{MOCU}(b)
&=\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u_b^*)-L(\theta,u_\theta^*)]\\
&=\min_{u\in\mathcal U}\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u)]
-\mathbb E_b[\min_{v\in\mathcal U}L(\theta,v)].
\label{eq:general_mocu}
\end{align}
Both actions use the same loss~\cite{yoon2013mocu,boluki2018generalized}.

\subsubsection{Control cost and shortfall}
Normalize the marginal injection cost to one. For a dimensionless
shortfall-penalty parameter $\lambda>1$, define
\begin{equation}
L(\theta,u)=u+\lambda[U(\theta)-u]_+,
\qquad (z)_+=\max(z,0).
\label{eq:operational_cost}
\end{equation}
The parameter $\lambda$ is a fixed loss preference; the surrogate measures
control expenditure and shortfall in common control units.
For known $\theta$, the loss has slopes $1-\lambda<0$ below $U(\theta)$ and
$1$ above it. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
u_\theta^*=U(\theta),\qquad L(\theta,u_\theta^*)=U(\theta).
\label{eq:model_specific_control}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Bayes action and quantile characterization}
Let $F_b(v)=\Pr_b(U\le v)$ and $\alpha=1/\lambda\in(0,1)$.
The convex function $J_b(u)=\mathbb E_b[L(\theta,u)]$ has left and right
derivatives $1-\lambda+\lambda F_b(u^-)$ and
$1-\lambda+\lambda F_b(u)$, respectively. Its minimizers satisfy
\begin{equation}
F_b(u^- )\le1-\alpha\le F_b(u).
\label{eq:quantile_optimality}
\end{equation}
The smallest minimizer, including for discrete beliefs, is
\begin{equation}
u_b^*=Q_{1-\alpha}(U\mid b)
=\inf\{v:F_b(v)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\label{eq:u_ctrl_support}
\end{equation}
The associated objective cost of uncertainty is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{OCU}(\theta,b)
=(u_b^*-U)_++(\lambda-1)(U-u_b^*)_+\ge0.
\label{eq:ocu}
\end{equation}
Thus over-control and under-control have marginal regret $1$ and
$\lambda-1$, and posterior MOCU is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MOCU}(b)=\mathbb E_b
\left[u_b^*+\lambda(U-u_b^*)_+-U\right].
\label{eq:belief_mocu}
\end{equation}
Under the threshold assumptions,
$\Pr_b(\mathsf{Safe}(\theta,u_b^*))=F_b(u_b^*)\ge1-\alpha$.
This is a posterior probability statement relative to the assumed model.

\subsubsection{Sequential objective and value of information}
Let $\Pi_T$ be the class of measurable history-dependent policies satisfying
the probe budget and no-repetition constraint. With histories generated by
$p_0$, the observation likelihood, and $\pi$, the design problem is
\begin{equation}
\pi^*\in\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi_T}
\mathbb E_{H_T\mid\pi}[\operatorname{MOCU}(b_T)].
\label{eq:mocu_policy}
\end{equation}
Here $b_T=p(\theta\mid H_T)$. The tower property gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_{H_T\mid\pi}[\operatorname{MOCU}(b_T)]
&=\mathbb E_{\theta,H_T\mid\pi}
[L(\theta,u_{b_T}^*)-U(\theta)],\\
\mathbb E_{H_T\mid\pi}\mathbb E_{b_T}[U]
&=\mathbb E_{p_0}[U].
\label{eq:mocu-tower}
\end{align}
Hence minimizing expected terminal MOCU is equivalent to minimizing
expected terminal operational loss. The expectation identities require the
posterior and history distribution to use the same generative model.

For any admissible next probe $\xi$, posterior updating satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{Y\mid b,\xi}[\operatorname{MOCU}(b^{\xi,Y})]
\le\operatorname{MOCU}(b).
\label{eq:mocu-information-value}
\end{equation}
The inequality follows by retaining $u_b^*$ after the observation and
applying the tower property. If an additional probe is feasible and has no
cost in the objective, the optimal expected MOCU cannot increase with the
budget. Neither strict adaptive gains nor improvement for every realized
history follows.

\subsection{Particle approximation}
The implementation represents this posterior with $N_{\mathrm p}$ particles
$\{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},w_n^{(t)}\}_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}$. Their weights
are updated by
\begin{equation}
w_n^{(t)}
=\frac{w_n^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)},\xi_t)}
{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm p}}w_j^{(t-1)}
p(\mathbf y_t\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(j)},\xi_t)}.
\label{eq:particle-update}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Terminal decision.}
Let $U_n$ denote the stored control requirement for particle $\theta^{(n)}$,
with normalized posterior weights $w_n$. The empirical CDF and decision are
\begin{align}
F_N(v)&=\sum_n w_n\mathbf1\{U_n\le v\},\\
q_N&=\inf\{v:F_N(v)\ge1-\alpha\},\\
u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(b)&=\operatorname{snap}_{\mathcal G}^{\uparrow}(q_N).
\label{eq:u_ctrl}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal G$ is the configured control grid. The aligned implementation
requires every $U_n\in\mathcal G$ and zero margin, so the empirical quantile
already lies on the grid and snapping does not change it. It is consequently
an exact IBR action for this finite particle-bank loss. If requirements lie
off-grid, rounding a continuous quantile upward is not generally the
unconstrained grid Bayes optimum; one must minimize expected loss over the
grid explicitly or retain continuous controls. The implementation checks
this alignment before running the primary experiment.

The bank-level objective used for training and posterior evaluation is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{MOCU}}_N(b)
=\sum_n w_n\{u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}+\lambda(U_n-u_{\mathrm{ctrl}})_+-U_n\}.
\label{eq:particle_mocu}
\end{equation}
All methods use the same bank, loss, quantile, and control range. Probe and
control bank rows must match in particle values and ordering, physics,
scenario, units, and simulator provenance. A dense control-grid bank is a
numerical approximation to the continuous physical requirements; its error
must be distinguished from posterior uncertainty.



\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Reduced-network construction}
Let $\mathcal A=\mathcal N\setminus\mathcal M$. For a connected lossless
network with fixed voltage magnitudes, let
$\mathbf L$ be the weighted susceptance Laplacian, ordered as
$(\mathcal M,\mathcal A)$. If $\mathcal A\ne\varnothing$, assume
$\mathbf L_{\mathcal A\mathcal A}$ is invertible and define
\begin{align}
\mathbf L_{\mathrm{red}}&=\mathbf L_{\mathcal M\mathcal M}
-\mathbf L_{\mathcal M\mathcal A}\mathbf L_{\mathcal A\mathcal A}^{-1}
\mathbf L_{\mathcal A\mathcal M},\\
\mathbf A_{\mathrm{inj}}&=
\left[\mathbf I_m\; -\mathbf L_{\mathcal M\mathcal A}
\mathbf L_{\mathcal A\mathcal A}^{-1}\right],\\
B_{ij}&=-(L_{\mathrm{red}})_{ij}\quad(i\ne j),\qquad B_{ii}=0.
\label{eq:kron-map}
\end{align}
The physical-bus input map is $\mathbf a(b)=\mathbf A_{\mathrm{inj}}\mathbf e_b$
in the stated ordering. With no algebraic buses, use $\mathbf L_{\mathrm{red}}=\mathbf L$
and $\mathbf A_{\mathrm{inj}}=\mathbf I_m$. The linear Kron map combined with
sinusoidal coupling is a reduced-model approximation. Network data follow
the IEEE test cases in MATPOWER~\cite{zimmerman2011matpower}.

\subsection{Held-out validation}
For an independent test system $\theta^\star$, evaluate the applied action
against a separately computed continuous physical oracle $U^*(\theta^\star)$:
\begin{equation}
R^*=u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}+\lambda[U^*(\theta^\star)-u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}]_+
-U^*(\theta^\star).
\label{eq:realized_regret}
\end{equation}
Report mean held-out $R^*$ alongside posterior bank MOCU as a separate
measure of operational performance. Their difference can reflect finite support,
control-grid approximation, and model or posterior miscalibration. Regret
against a continuous oracle includes control discretization error and should
not be described as purely uncertainty cost for a restricted grid action
class. A strictly grid-relative MOCU instead subtracts
$\min_{v\in\mathcal G}L(\theta,v)$ for each model.

Under the threshold model and exact posterior, the quantile ensures posterior
coverage $\Pr_b(U\le u_b^*)\ge1-\alpha$. It does not protect every posterior
model and is not a guaranteed empirical safety rate. Report true-system
nadir and ROCOF violations, safety counts and confidence intervals, control
magnitude, and regret separately. Any 95\% safety eligibility rule must be
prespecified; retain results for ineligible methods and distinguish a point
estimate passing 95\% from statistical evidence for that claim. The finite
loss is scientifically a MOCU specialization, while its engineering adequacy
depends on the stated loss preference, physical validation, and calibration.

\section{Discussion}
Discuss when adaptation and non-myopic planning improve the downstream
objective, why Fixed can be competitive, why EIG and MOCU can disagree, and how
horizon and system size affect performance. Limitations include simulated
Kron-reduced networks, prescribed priors, fixed probe amplitude, discrete
actions, simplified terminal control, and three training seeds. Reduced-model $K_i$ allocations and parameter priors are study assumptions.
The paper-based IEEE30 model requires a different dynamic representation.
The finite-loss coefficient needs sensitivity analysis and does not quantify
actual outage damage; empirical calibration and physical safety must be
validated separately. Adaptive branching and improvement over Myopic and
Fixed are empirical hypotheses, not prerequisites imposed on the results.
